# Family Story — one-page definition (docs/FAMILY_STORY.md)

The built half of the Meta entry. Decided 2026-09-22; the F-steps in
docs/plans/chris.md and docs/plans/justin.md implement exactly this.

## What it is

Every morning, Irin sends consented family members a plain-language story
of the night instead of raw data, at a disclosure level the patient
controls. Thesis: today the only way to reassure your family is to give
them your medical data stream; Family Story gives them peace of mind
without the numbers.

## Who sees it, and how it arrives

Family members the patient adds by name and email. Primary channel is
email, reusing the morning report's existing SMTP path, so the built tier
needs zero new infrastructure. Stretch tier: a story card at the top of
frontend/family/, fetched from the relay with a bearer token, above the
existing read-only view. Two disclosure levels, chosen per recipient:

- **Level 1 "Story only"**: the narrative, no family-view token, no
  numbers.
- **Level 2 "Story + view"**: the narrative plus the existing family page.

The family view itself is unchanged; Family Story sits in front of it, it
does not replace it.
The updated Rounds spec (2026-09-25) recommends cutting the family view
for the hackathon; it is kept for this entry (decision 26), never shown in
the Impiricus slot, with a revocable family bearer from Irin Cloud's
rollup (no Nightscout token anywhere in page source).

## What it says

Two to three sentences in family register, generated by the existing
narrative pipeline with a family prompt (narrative.py, task
family_story: Muse Spark through Backboard's OpenRouter provider or the
Meta Model API direct, then the direct Claude call, then the template,
the validator after every link; decision 29): how the night went, whether
anything happened, and that the patient handled it. Always frame the
patient's agency ("he caught it and treated it"), never alarm, never
medical advice. Honesty rules carry over from the device: a rough night is
told calmly but truthfully ("a long low around 3 AM, treated, back in
range by 4"), and a no-data night says "Irin didn't have data last
night," never "all fine." Optionally ends with one conversation starter
that is not about glucose. Save celebrations are NOT part of the automatic
story; sharing a save is its own patient-initiated action (Save Shared),
so the automatic channel never discloses more than the level promises.

## The voice note (added 2026-09-25)

Each story is also rendered once by ElevenLabs in the device's voice, a
20-second mp3 attached to the email and playable from the app's morning
screen; a Level 1 story's audio is generated from the validated text, so
it can never carry a number the text does not. Demo-mode stories render
the clip locally, badged DEMO, on the morning screen only, and are never
emailed, so the video's morning shot is reproducible on demand.

## Opt-in and control

The patient adds each recipient, picks their level, and chooses send
mode: automatic, or approve-each-morning. Default: the first story
requires approval, then automatic. The patient's own morning screen shows
exactly what was sent and to whom ("Sent to Mom"), with one-tap pause and
instant revoke per recipient. Recipients can unsubscribe from any email.
Consent state lives in Settings.

## Validator

Reuses the narrative validator with the rule inverted per level: Level 1
stories must contain ZERO glucose values (mg/dL numbers banned; clock
times allowed); any violation falls back to the deterministic family
template. Demo rule inherits invariant 1: stories generated in demo mode
are badged DEMO and are never emailed to real recipients (same class of
rule as "demo treatments never post to real Nightscout").

## Demo beat (video, about 20 seconds)

Split shot: a phone showing a raw CGM-follow graph with a worried face,
versus Mom's phone showing the story. Line: "Same night. Two ways to find
out your son is okay." Then the patient's morning screen: "Sent to Mom,"
and the pause button, to show who's in control.
